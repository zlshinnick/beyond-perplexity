The central finding

Reasoning starts before language.

A brief warm-up on a simple abstract task based on stack memory operations improves multi-hop reasoning, with gains concentrated on sequential composition. In controlled experiments, it also accelerates learning of longer reasoning chains. These effects persist after billions of language-training tokens without a corresponding improvement in language-modeling loss.

Faster learning on controlled multi-hop queries.

DEPO asks a model to follow one to four links through a directed cycle. After STACK and language pretraining, the evaluated four-layer models learn this task faster and reach near-perfect accuracy at all tested hop counts. The language-only baselines still struggle with four-hop queries at the end of fine-tuning.

Initial exposure produces the largest gain.

Mean change in F1 across the three QA benchmarks at each tested insertion point (Figure 9). The initial +2.32 is the mean of the three QA gains reported above. STACK uses a separate optimizer; intermediate insertions pause language pretraining and its learning-rate schedule, which then resume.

More exposure is not always better. Adding three further STACK phases improves HotpotQA and 2WikiMultiHopQA but reduces MuSiQue F1, while increasing the abstract-training budget (paper, Figure 11).

QA gains persist through language pretraining.

At matched language-token counts, separately fine-tuned SmolLM-135M checkpoints retain higher QA F1 with initial STACK training. This advantage is present at every evaluated checkpoint, through the full 2.69B-token language-pretraining budget.

Implications and scope

Evaluate specific capabilities alongside perplexity.

Similar language-modeling loss can conceal differences in downstream learning. Here, the effects of abstract training depend on the task’s structure, the capability being evaluated, and when exposure occurs.

Scope of the evidence. The natural-language QA experiments use SmolLM-135M and SmolLM-360M; the controlled experiments use a separate four-layer model. The findings do not establish benefits at larger scales or across all tasks. How abstract training changes the learned computations remains an open question.
